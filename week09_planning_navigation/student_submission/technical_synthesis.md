A route is suitable for a robot sharing space with people if it can balance efficiency, navigation accuracy, and safety for the people within the environment. Just because a robot is fast or slow does not mean it's a good robot, it needs to be able to avoid obstacles, not take any unnecessary detours and evaluate a proper route algorithm that does not put pedestrians at risk or make them feel uncomfortable. Shorter routes may sound nice but could pass really close to people or obstacles, a longer route may sound worse but could be the safest as it doesn't take sharp turns and ensures that it stays predictable. To also search effectively and not explore unnecessarily and find a good route.  Route quality, some path's may look good but is there a better one that isn't exploring unnecessary routes or doing random backtracking over and over again. Being able to avoid objects on a map and detect if a route is even possible in the first place based on a map or if there were moving humans, can it maintain reasonable spacing to ensure safety, can it adjust its directions if a pedestrian is suddenly in front of them without causing harm and still remaining predictable. Having all these conditions is what makes a route good for a robot, it need's to be somewhat efficient but it cannot trade safety for efficiency.
